About the Tracker

Ukraine War Unmanned Systems Tracker

An independent open-source research project tracking drone warfare, naval drone operations, ground robot deployments, and Russian losses in Ukraine. The data presented here is compiled and curated from publicly available sources — not independently generated. The analytical work on top of it is original.

What this is

The tracker is a structured database of open-source intelligence on unmanned systems in the Russia-Ukraine war, maintained alongside doctoral research at King's College London. Its value lies in the curation, structuring, and analysis of data that exists across hundreds of disparate public sources — not in any claim to original data collection.

All primary data comes from Ukrainian government bodies, open-source researchers, verified video evidence, and credible media reporting. Every entry carries source citations. Where sources conflict, the more conservative assessment is applied.

What this isn't

This tracker does not conduct original fieldwork, interview combatants, or have access to classified or government-restricted information. It is not affiliated with any military, government, or intelligence organisation. It does not claim to be comprehensive — particularly for recent events where open-source coverage is still developing.

The original analyses in the Analyses and Deep Strike Research tabs are working findings — data-driven but not peer-reviewed. They should be treated as research-in-progress, not authoritative conclusions.

Methodology
Inclusion criteria

Events are included where there is at least one credible open-source report — an official Ukrainian military or intelligence statement, verified video evidence, or reporting by two or more independent outlets. Russian acknowledgement is noted but not required.

Damage classification

Sunk = total loss on seabed. Destroyed = total loss confirmed. Damaged = partial damage, target repairable. Shot Down = aerial asset destroyed in flight. Disrupted = operation temporarily affected.

Uncertainty & limitations

Active conflict reporting is inherently incomplete. Russian-side data systematically underreports losses. Ukrainian official figures may overstate. All quantitative analyses carry methodology notes flagging known limitations, sample size constraints, and confounding variables.

How to cite this work

This tracker curates open-source data rather than generating primary data. When citing figures from the tracker, please cite both this resource and the underlying primary source where possible. The primary source is always listed on each entry.

The original analytical work — the corridor analysis, saturation findings, interception rate analyses, and Logistics Lockdown research — is the intellectual contribution of this project and should be attributed accordingly.

Citing the tracker (general)
Mathewson, A. (2024–2026). Ukraine War Unmanned Systems Tracker. King's College London, Department of War Studies. Retrieved [date] from https://unmannedsystemstracker.com
Citing the original analyses
Mathewson, A. (2025). '[Analysis title]'. In Ukraine War Unmanned Systems Tracker. King's College London, Department of War Studies. https://unmannedsystemstracker.com/#analyses
Citing underlying primary data
When citing air defence figures, cite Petro Ivaniuk / Ukrainian Air Force as the primary source. For KIA figures, cite Mediazona / BBC News. For equipment losses, cite Oryx. For territorial data, cite Black Bird Group / DeepState. For UAV kill board data, cite USF Pidrakhuyka. This tracker compiles these sources and should be credited as such, not as a replacement for citing the originators.
For media use
Journalists may cite the tracker and its analyses freely with attribution. For comment, context, or interview requests, contact via the Sources tab. The tracker is available for research collaboration and data partnership — please get in touch to discuss.
